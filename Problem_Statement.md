An Emotion-Aware Study Companion for Adaptive Academic Support
Academic self-study is one of the most emotionally volatile activities students engage in, yet it is also the one where they are most alone. When working through difficult material, students routinely cycle through frustration when a concept refuses to click, anger at repeated failure, and helplessness that compounds into disengagement — emotional states that directly degrade memory consolidation, problem-solving capacity, and motivation. As assessments approach, this frustration frequently transforms into anxiety, manifesting as fear-based avoidance, catastrophic thinking, and the inability to recall material that was previously understood. Despite the well-established relationship between emotional state and learning outcome, current AI-based study tools respond identically to every student in every emotional condition: they deliver the next answer, the next hint, the next question — with no awareness of whether the student in front of them is thriving, struggling, or on the verge of shutting the laptop and walking away.
This project addresses that gap. We propose an emotion-aware study buddy chatbot that uses real-time facial emotion recognition, via a webcam and a custom-trained convolutional neural network on the AffectNet-HQ dataset, to continuously monitor the student's emotional state during a self-study session. When the system detects frustration-linked expressions — anger, disgust, contempt, or sadness — it shifts the chatbot's behaviour toward patient, scaffolded explanation, encouragement, and pacing adjustments. When fear-linked expressions characteristic of exam anxiety are detected, the system transitions to a calming, confidence-building interaction mode that reframes difficulty, normalises imperfection, and guides the student's attention back to what they know. Unlike existing tutoring chatbots that rely solely on what the student types, this system treats the face as an additional, honest signal of cognitive and emotional load — one that cannot be edited before sending.
The target population is university students engaged in independent study, a group that is both large and consistently underserved by emotionally intelligent tools, with approximately 40,000 students enrolled at the University of Auckland alone representing an immediately accessible and evaluable cohort. By integrating real-time emotion detection with a large language model whose prompt and behaviour are dynamically conditioned on the detected emotional state, this system aims to reduce study-induced emotional disengagement and lower the affective barriers that prevent students from performing at their potential.
